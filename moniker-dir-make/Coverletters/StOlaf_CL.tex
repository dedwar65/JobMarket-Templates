\opening{Dear Members of the Search Committee,}

I am writing to express my interest in the one-year Term Assistant Professor (or Visiting Instructor) position in Economics at St.\ Olaf College, beginning August 17, 2026. I am a Ph.D.\ candidate in Economics at Johns Hopkins University, advised by Professors Christopher D.\ Carroll, Nicholas W.\ Papageorge, and Stelios Fourakis, and I expect to receive my degree in May 2026. My research fields are macroeconomics, household finance, inequality, and applied econometrics.

My research agenda focuses on the determinants of wealth inequality and the mechanisms through which heterogeneity in returns to wealth shapes aggregate outcomes. In my job market paper, I estimate heterogeneity in household portfolio returns using the Survey of Consumer Finances and calibrate a structural model in which households face persistent differences in returns. I show that this form of heterogeneity substantially improves the model’s ability to match observed wealth concentration and helps connect micro-level return dispersion to macroeconomic inequality.

In a second project, I use the Health and Retirement Study to study the relationship between trust and household portfolio performance. I document a hump-shaped relationship between trust and returns to net wealth and estimate the persistent component of individual returns in panel data. In a third project, I develop a decision-theoretic framework for ranking wealth distributions under ambiguity and use it to study the relationship between inequality aversion and the Black-White median wealth gap in the United States.

St.\ Olaf’s commitment to rigorous liberal-arts teaching and close faculty-student engagement is an excellent fit for my goals as a teacher-scholar. I am especially excited by this position’s emphasis on Principles and senior electives in Trade, Development, and Gender (or closely related fields), as well as the opportunity to contribute an additional elective in my area.

\textbf{Teaching.} My teaching experience includes undergraduate macroeconomics and an upper-level macro policy seminar, where I led weekly discussion sections and held regular office hours for classes ranging from 16 to 40 students. My teaching approach is student-centered: I pair clear conceptual scaffolding with applied exercises and structured office-hour support so students can move from initial uncertainty to independent problem-solving. At St.\ Olaf, I am prepared to teach Principles of Economics and upper-level electives that connect to Development and Gender through inequality, household decision-making, and policy evaluation. I would also be glad to teach an urban-economics-oriented elective, including topics such as spatial inequality, housing and household balance sheets, and local opportunity gaps, adapted to department priorities.

I would be excited to contribute to St.\ Olaf’s Economics Department through engaged teaching, active scholarship, and mentoring of undergraduate researchers. More broadly, I value St.\ Olaf’s emphasis on inclusive, globally engaged liberal-arts education and would welcome the opportunity to support students with diverse interests and backgrounds.

I would be honored to join St.\ Olaf’s Department of Economics for the 2026--27 academic year. Thank you for your consideration.
